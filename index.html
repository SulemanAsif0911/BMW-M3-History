Chapter 01 — 1986
01 / 04
Where it began

BMW M3
E30

Group A touring car rules forced BMW's hand: build a road car worthy of the racetrack, or don't race at all. The result was flared arches, a reworked chassis, and a screaming four-cylinder derived from BMW Motorsport's own competition engines. It won on Sunday and was sold on Monday — and in doing so, invented a nameplate.

Chapter 02 — 2001
02 / 04
The homologation outlaw

BMW M3
GTR

To beat Porsche in the American Le Mans Series, BMW did the unthinkable and dropped a purpose-built V8 into a car that had only ever run a straight-six. The GTR won seven of ten races and the 2001 GT championship on debut — then triggered a rule change that ended its own eligibility within a single season.

Chapter 03 — Aftermarket
03 / 04
The street tribute

M3 GTR
AC Schnitzer Black Edition

Not every GTR story was written inside BMW Motorsport. Independent tuners took the E46's race-bred silhouette and pushed it further — a wider stance, deeper aerodynamics, and a blacked-out finish built for the road rather than the pit lane. It's the aftermarket's own answer to a car BMW never fully let go of racing.

The first M3 wagon

BMW M3 Competition
Touring

Nearly four decades after the E30, BMW finally built the M3 enthusiasts had asked for since the '90s: a wagon. The G81 pairs a twin-turbo inline-six with xDrive all-wheel drive and a full cargo hold, proving that a homologation bloodline and a school run aren't mutually exclusive.
